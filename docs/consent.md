## What this study is

MorsEye is a student research project at BITS Pilani. It measures how well an ordinary laptop webcam can tell
deliberate eye closures apart from normal blinks, and how fast people can type Morse code with their eyes.

## What you will do

You will sit in front of a laptop for about 12 minutes. You will look at a dot, read some text, and close your eyes
or wink when cues appear on the screen. There are no right or wrong answers. You can take a break at any time.

## What is recorded

- **Always:** numbers that describe your eyes in each video frame (how open each eye is, 12 points around the eyes,
  and the angle of your head), and the times when cues appeared. No picture of your face is stored.
- **Only if you tick the separate video box:** a video of your face, kept on this laptop only, so the researcher can
  mark exactly when each blink started and ended. It is deleted as soon as labelling is finished.
- Your age range, whether you wear glasses, and the lighting and camera used. **Not** your name.

Your data is stored under a code (such as P07), never your name. Any list linking codes to names is kept on paper,
offline, and destroyed at the end of the project.

## How the data is used

The eye numbers (not video) may be published as an open dataset and summarised in a research paper. Nobody will be
able to see your face from them.

## Your rights

- Taking part is voluntary. There is no payment.
- You can stop at any time without giving a reason.
- You can ask for your data to be deleted later by quoting your code. Data already published in summary form cannot be
  removed from the summary, but your rows will be removed from the dataset.
- You must be 18 or older to take part.
