## 0. TL;DR

MorsEye today is a 242-line browser demo that turns eye closures into Morse code. It has no evaluation, it types a dot
for most natural blinks, its threshold is fixed for everyone, it needs a keyboard to be "hands-free", and its README
describes a different program from the one that runs.

MorsEye 2.0 has three deliverables:

1. **A research study.** It measures how well a consumer webcam can separate *deliberate* from *spontaneous* blinks,
   how much a few seconds of personal calibration helps (a few-shot problem, which links it to FewGaze), and how fast
   and accurately people can type with it.
2. **A rebuilt app and a new website.** It has calibration, natural-blink rejection, blink commands (delete, space),
   speech output, a study mode that records data, and a designed, animated site that explains the method and shows
   the results interactively.
3. **A short paper** (4–6 pages, ACM format) with the study, figures and an honest limitations section, plus a public
   dataset of *features only* (no faces).

## 1. Goals and non-goals

### Goals
- G1. Replace "it works on my face" with **measured numbers**: detection F1, false triggers per minute, characters per
  minute, error rate, all with confidence intervals across participants.
- G2. Make the app **actually hands-free**: no keyboard or mouse needed after setup.
- G3. A website that a CaCTüS reviewer opens and understands within 30 seconds: what it is, how it works, what was
  found, and the limits.
- G4. A paper whose every number is regenerated by one script from the logged data.
- G5. Work whose **authorship is recorded honestly**: data collected and labelled by Saksham, and every part the AI
  agent drafted listed in `AI_USE.md` (§2).

### Non-goals
- No clinical claims. Participants will be able-bodied volunteers. The paper must say this.
- No server, accounts or analytics. All processing stays on the device.
- No publishing of participant faces or raw video, ever.
- No native mobile app. A responsive web app is enough.
- Light/LED mode is not part of the study (see §5.9).

## 3. The research

### 3.1 Working title
**"Telling deliberate from spontaneous blinks on a webcam: few-shot calibration for blink-based Morse text entry"**

### 3.2 Research questions
- **RQ1 (detection).** On a consumer webcam (around 30 fps), how accurately can landmark-based detectors find
  *deliberate* blinks while ignoring *spontaneous* ones? How much does per-user calibration with *k* examples
  (k = 0, 1, 3, 5, 10) improve this?
- **RQ2 (timing).** What are the duration distributions of spontaneous blinks, deliberate "short" closures and deliberate
  "long" closures? What dot/dash/reject thresholds do they imply? How much does frame rate limit timing precision?
- **RQ3 (text entry).** What entry rate and error rate do novices reach with the improved system, and how do they change
  over three short blocks of practice?

### 3.3 Hypotheses (to be committed before any data collection)
- H1. Calibrated detectors (k ≥ 3) have a higher event-level F1 than the fixed EAR threshold of 0.21.
- H2. MediaPipe blendshape scores (`eyeBlinkLeft/Right`) detect closures at least as well as EAR from 6 landmarks.
- H3. A minimum-duration reject window (spontaneous blinks are short) cuts false characters per minute during reading
  by at least half compared with the current app.
- H4. *(Exploratory.)* Wink mode (one eye only) produces fewer false triggers than both-eye mode, at the cost of
  higher effort.
- H5. Entry rate rises and error rate falls from block 1 to block 3.

These are written into `docs/preregistration.md` (agent draft, Saksham approves, §2) and committed **before the first participant**. Later changes
go in a "Deviations" section with dates. Do not edit history.

### 3.4 Detectors to compare (all run offline on the same recorded features)

| ID | Signal | Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Mean EAR of both eyes | fixed 0.21 | The current app, as a baseline |
| D2 | Mean EAR | per-user, from k calibration closures: `thr = open_median − α·(open_median − closed_median)`, α = 0.5 | k ∈ {1,3,5,10} |
| D3 | Blendshape `max(eyeBlinkLeft, eyeBlinkRight)` | fixed 0.5 | MediaPipe FaceLandmarker |
| D4 | Blendshape | per-user, same formula as D2 | |
| D5 | Wink: one eye closed (blendshape) while the other is open (score < 0.3) | per-user | Exploratory |

All detectors use **hysteresis** (enter "closed" below `thr`, leave above `thr + h`, with h = 10% of the open–closed
range) and **no majority-vote buffer**. The current 5-frame vote (`script.js:46`) adds about 80–170 ms of lag.
Durations are measured from the **first closed frame to the first open frame**, using frame timestamps from the
video, not `performance.now()` at callback time.

Few-shot calibration evaluation: for each participant, draw *k* calibration closures at random from their calibration
block, 20 random draws per k, fit the threshold, then evaluate on that participant's *other* blocks. Report the mean
and 95% CI across participants. This is deliberately parallel to FewGaze's label-budget design.

### 3.5 Study A: blink detection benchmark (must be done by 13 Nov)

> **Design change, 24 Oct 2026.** Without recruited participants, Studies A and B run as a single-person case study
> (the author, recorded repeatedly across days and lighting), plus a check of natural blinks on public video
> datasets of other people. `docs/preregistration.md` and `docs/public-data.md` hold the current design; the
> multi-person plan below is kept for reference and is used again if volunteers take part.
- **Participants:** at least 8 (aim for 10–12) adult volunteers. Record age range, glasses (yes/no), lighting, camera
  model and **measured** fps. No names in any data file. Use IDs P01, P02, …
- **Setup:** laptop webcam, around 50–70 cm away, normal room light. Record each person's setup in the session log.
- **Protocol** (runs inside the app's Study Mode, §5.7, about 12 min per person):
  1. Setup screen: ID, session, seed, setup notes, and whether to record video (§3.8).
  2. *Calibration block* (1 min): 10 cued "short closures", 10 cued "long closures", 15 s eyes open looking at a dot.
  3. *Spontaneous block* (3 min): read a neutral text on screen, no task. This yields natural blinks only.
  4. *Deliberate block* (3 min): randomised cues "SHORT" / "LONG" / "WINK-L" / "WINK-R", 40 cues, 2–4 s apart with jitter.
  5. *Mixed block* (3 min): read text, and when a cue appears in the corner, respond to it. This is the realistic case.
- **What is recorded:** per frame, the video timestamp, the 6 EAR landmarks for each eye, EAR left/right, the 52
  blendshape scores (or at least the eye ones), head yaw/pitch/roll from the transformation matrix, and a face-found
  flag. Cue onset times are also logged. **Video** is recorded locally, *only if the participant ticks the separate
  video checkbox* on the setup screen, and only for labelling. It is never published.
- **Ground truth [HUMAN]:** in the Labelling Tool (§5.8), mark every closure's onset and offset and its type
  (spontaneous / deliberate-short / deliberate-long / wink-L / wink-R). Cues help, but the human label is the truth.
  To check yourself, re-label two participants a week later and report your agreement with yourself (Cohen's κ for
  type, mean absolute onset difference in ms).
- **Metrics:**
  - Event-level precision, recall and F1 for *deliberate* closures. A detection matches a labelled event if the
    intervals overlap with IoU ≥ 0.3.
  - **False triggers per minute in the spontaneous block.** This is the headline number for H3.
  - Duration error (detected minus labelled) in ms, compared with the frame interval.
  - Short/long classification accuracy and the confusion matrix.
- **Statistics:** per-participant metrics, then the mean with a 95% bootstrap CI over participants (10,000 resamples).
  Use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for paired detector comparisons, with Holm correction. With N around 10, say plainly
  that the power is low.

### 3.6 Study B: text entry (by 13 Nov if possible; otherwise December)
- Same participants if possible, in a separate 20-minute session.
- **System:** the best detector from Study A with thresholds fixed in advance by Saksham from the Study A distributions **[HUMAN]**.
- **Phrases:** 15 short phrases from the MacKenzie & Soukoreff (2003) phrase set, lower-case, 3 blocks of 5 phrases.
  Show the Morse reference card on screen.
- **Metrics:**
  - Entry rate in characters per minute (CPM) and WPM = CPM / 5, timed from the first closure to the last character.
  - **Error rate:** MSD-based character error rate (Soukoreff & MacKenzie, 2003), both uncorrected and corrected.
  - Closures per character, including deletions.
  - Raw NASA-TLX after each block (6 sliders in the app).
- **Analysis:** the change across blocks (paired, per participant) with CIs. Optionally a learning-curve fit; keep it
  simple.

### 3.7 Study C (optional, December): practice over days
The same 3–5 people do 10 minutes a day for 5 days. Plot the entry-rate learning curve. If used, frame it with the
CaCTüS Project 9 idea that motor behaviour becomes more automatic with practice. Only cite their paper if you read it.

### 3.8 Ethics and data handling
- No ethics review is sought (decided by Saksham, 24 Oct 2026): the only participant is the author, and the public
  videos are used under their published licence. The paper says so. If volunteers take part later, revisit this first.
- No consent form (decided by Saksham, 25 Oct 2026): the only participant is the author. If volunteers take part
  later, write a consent form and add a consent screen to Study Mode first.
- Data files hold only IDs. Keep a mapping from ID to name, if any, offline on paper.
- **Public dataset** (`data/public/`): per-frame features and labels only. No images, no video, no landmarks beyond the
  12 eye points. Licence CC BY 4.0. Add a datasheet `data/DATASHEET.md` describing who, how many, devices and lighting.
- The paper and site must say: "Participants were N able-bodied adult volunteers. The system has not been tested with
  people with motor impairments, and no clinical claim is made."

### 3.9 Threats to validity (these go in the paper)
Small N; Saksham is both the experimenter and the labeller (labeller bias; self-agreement κ partly addresses this);
convenience sample (friends or classmates); one or two camera models; deliberate blinks were cued, not natural intent;
able-bodied users only; the in-the-lab reading task.

## 5. App requirements (the "Try it" tool)

### 5.1 Stack
- **Vite + TypeScript** (strict), multi-page build. No framework is required. Use small modules.
- `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` (FaceLandmarker with `outputFaceBlendshapes: true`, `outputFacialTransformationMatrixes: true`, `runningMode: "VIDEO"`), pinned to an exact version. Self-host `face_landmarker.task` under `public/models/`.
- Animation: GSAP 3 with ScrollTrigger (pinned exact version) for the site; CSS and the Web Animations API for the app.
- Charts on the site: D3 v7 (pinned) or Observable Plot (pinned).
- Tests: Vitest. Lint: ESLint + Prettier. CI: a GitHub Action running build, test and lint.
- Deploy: Cloudflare Pages (project `morseye`, https://morseye.pages.dev).

### 5.2 Module layout
```
src/
  capture/camera.ts          getUserMedia, fps measurement, frame timestamps
  vision/landmarker.ts       FaceLandmarker wrapper -> FrameFeatures
  vision/features.ts         EAR (6-point, per eye), blendshape pick, head pose from matrix
  detect/detector.ts         Detector interface + D1..D5 implementations, hysteresis
  detect/calibration.ts      open/closed stats, threshold fit, k-shot logic
  segment/segmenter.ts       closure events -> {reject|dot|dash|cmd} with durations
  decode/decoder.ts          Morse state machine, letter/word gaps, commands, MORSE table
  output/speech.ts           speechSynthesis wrapper (letter + word)
  output/suggest.ts          (optional) word completion from a small frequency list
  study/protocol.ts          cue schedules for Study A/B, seeded RNG
  study/recorder.ts          per-frame log + events -> JSONL/CSV, local download
  ui/...                     components (meter, ring, tape, cards)
analysis/                    Python: load logs, label join, metrics, stats, figures
labeler/                     the labelling tool page (§5.8)
```
`FrameFeatures` = `{t_ms, face: bool, earL, earR, blinkL, blinkR, yaw, pitch, roll}`. Every downstream module consumes
only this, so the analysis can replay recorded logs through **exactly the same TypeScript code** (export a Node build of
`detect/` + `segment/` + `decode/`) or a line-for-line Python port tested against it.

### 5.3 Segmenter and decoder state machine
Defaults (to be **replaced by Saksham's chosen values after Study A [HUMAN]**):

| Closure duration | Meaning |
|---|---|
| < `REJECT_MS` (start: 200 ms) | Ignored as a probable spontaneous blink |
| `REJECT_MS` – `DOT_MAX_MS` (start: 600 ms) | dot |
| `DOT_MAX_MS` – `DASH_MAX_MS` (start: 1400 ms) | dash |
| ≥ `CMD_MS` (start: 2000 ms) | **backspace** (delete the last character, or the current partial letter) |

| Pause after last closure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ≥ `LETTER_GAP_MS` (start: 1200 ms) | Commit the current letter |
| ≥ `WORD_GAP_MS` (start: 3000 ms) | Insert a space (only once; no double spaces) |

Other commands:
- Wink-left (when wink mode is enabled): space. Wink-right: speak the current sentence.
- **Pause toggle:** two long closures within 4 s turns input on or off, so the user can rest without typing.
- An unknown pattern gets a visible `?` flash, is spoken as "unknown", and is not inserted into the text (this replaces the current `[..-.?]` insertion).

Presets: Slow / Medium / Fast (all values ×1.5 / ×1 / ×0.7). A "Custom" panel has sliders. All settings and the
calibration are saved in `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch).

Live feedback while the eye is closed: a **progress ring** fills over time and changes colour as it crosses
each band (grey = reject, blue = dot, amber = dash, red = delete). The user can *see* when a dot becomes a dash. This
matters most for usability. Each committed symbol gives a short sound (optional, off by default) and a visual tick.

### 5.4 Calibration flow
1. "Look at the dot and keep your eyes open" for 5 s: open stats (median, MAD).
2. "Close your eyes when the circle turns blue" ×5, 1 s each: closed stats.
3. Fit the threshold (§3.4). Show a live EAR/blendshape trace with the fitted line, and let the user try 5 practice blinks
   with green ticks.
4. Quality check: if `open_median − closed_median` is too small (poor light, glasses glare), show advice and allow a
   retry.
5. Store it per browser. A "Recalibrate" button is always visible.

### 5.5 Output
- A large decoded-text area (≥ 32 px), a current-pattern display (`·−·`), and the last letter shown huge for a moment.
- `speechSynthesis` speaks each letter (toggle) and each finished word (toggle).
- Copy text button. "Clear" needs a confirmation step built into the page (no `confirm()`).
- An `aria-live="polite"` region announces committed letters for screen readers.

### 5.6 Face and camera states
`NO_CAMERA` (permission help), `NO_FACE` (centre your face), `TOO_DARK` (from mean brightness), `MULTIPLE_FACES`,
`LOW_FPS` (< 20 fps: warn that timing is coarse; show the measured fps at all times in a small corner chip).

### 5.7 Study Mode (`/study`, hidden from the main nav)
- An experimenter screen enters the participant ID, session, block order seed and camera notes.
- A checkbox on the setup screen for the optional local video.
- Runs the Study A and B protocols from §3.5/3.6 with full-screen cues. Large text, a 3-2-1 lead-in, and progress.
- Records `FrameFeatures` for **every frame** plus cue and event logs, and optionally the video (`MediaRecorder`, webm).
- At the end, **the experimenter downloads** `P07_A_2026-11-03.jsonl` (+ `.webm`). Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
- A NASA-TLX form after each Study B block.
- A deterministic seed per participant is stored in the log.

### 5.8 Labelling Tool (`/labeler`, local only)
- Load a `.webm` + `.jsonl`. A video player synced to the EAR and blendshape traces on a zoomable timeline.
- Keyboard: `[` onset, `]` offset, `1–5` type, `←/→` frame step, `Del` remove.
- Pre-fills candidate events from cues (shown dashed). **Only human-confirmed events are exported** as ground truth.
- Exports `P07_A_labels.csv` with columns `onset_ms, offset_ms, type, labeller, labelled_at`.
- Shows the labelling time spent (report it in the paper).

### 5.9 Light/LED mode
Remove it from the product UI. Mention it in `legacy/` only. It distracts from the research story.

### 5.10 Performance and privacy budgets
- The Try page reaches its first frame processed in < 4 s on a mid-range laptop on 4G. The model loads lazily, only on
  `/try`, `/study` and `/labeler`.
- At least 25 fps processing on a laptop with integrated graphics. Show the measured fps.
- No network requests after load except fonts. No analytics. The privacy statement is on every page footer.

### 5.11 Tests (Vitest)
- `segmenter`: synthetic closure sequences → expected symbols, including edge cases exactly at each threshold.
- `decoder`: "SOS", "HELLO WORLD", unknown pattern, backspace in the middle of a letter, double word gap, pause toggle.
- `calibration`: a known open/closed distribution gives the expected threshold; the degenerate case shows a warning.
- `features`: EAR on hand-built landmark points (an open eye about 0.3, a closed eye about 0.05).
- A replay test: feed a recorded fixture `.jsonl` through the pipeline and compare with a stored expected output.

### 6.3 Visual direction: "telegraph tape"
Ground it in the subject. Morse lived on **paper telegraph tape** with punched or inked marks; MorsEye turns an eyelid into
the key that marks the tape.
- **Motif:** a horizontal tape strip that runs across sections. Dots and dashes appear on it as the eye "keys" them,
  and letters resolve underneath.
- **Palette (tokens, both themes):**
  - light: `--paper #F3F5F2` (cool, not cream), `--ink #13202B`, `--muted #5B6873`, `--tape #E4E9E3`,
    `--signal #D9480F` (key/closure colour, used sparingly), `--trace #1C7C8C` (EAR traces and charts).
  - dark: `--paper #0E1419`, `--ink #E6ECEF`, `--muted #92A0AA`, `--tape #1A232A`, `--signal #FF7A45`, `--trace #4FC1D1`.
  - Also an explicit **high-contrast theme** (pure black/white + yellow signal), as a toggle in the header.
- **Type:** Atkinson Hyperlegible for body text (designed by the Braille Institute for readability, which suits the
  subject); Bricolage Grotesque for headings; JetBrains Mono for Morse, numbers and data. All are Google Fonts with
  proper fallbacks. Body text at least 18 px, line length 60–70 characters.
- **Avoid:** purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism, emoji as icons, centred-everything layouts, stock eye photos. **Never
  use a real person's face anywhere on the site.** All face visuals are drawn from landmark points.

### 6.4 Signature animation: the hero
An SVG **eye drawn from about 30 landmark points** (upper lid, lower lid, iris). It blinks out `HI` in Morse on a loop:
`···· ··`.
- Each closure animates the upper lid down along the landmark path. Its duration equals the real dot/dash timing from
  §5.3, so the hero is the spec.
- At each closure, a mark is **stamped onto the tape** below (a dot or dash, inked in `--signal`), and the tape scrolls
  left.
- At each letter gap, the letter fades in under its marks.
- Under the eye, a small live EAR trace draws in step with the lids, with the threshold line shown.
- Controls: pause/play (visible, keyboard-focusable). With `prefers-reduced-motion`, show a static frame with the tape
  already filled.
- Implementation: GSAP timeline generated from a `{symbols, timings}` object, so changing the thresholds updates the
  animation.

### 6.5 `/how`: a scroll story (pinned, 6 steps, GSAP ScrollTrigger)
1. **Face to points.** About 470 landmark dots fade in to form a face, using a stored *synthetic/average* landmark set, not a
   participant's. Caption: what FaceLandmarker gives, and that this runs on the device.
2. **Eye to six points.** Zoom to one eye. The six EAR points light up. The vertical and horizontal distances draw as
   lines, and the formula `EAR = (|p2−p6| + |p3−p5|) / (2·|p1−p4|)` typesets beside them, each term highlighting with its line.
3. **Signal over time.** An EAR trace draws left to right from a real recorded snippet (features only). A spontaneous
   blink and a deliberate closure are annotated. The threshold line slides from the fixed 0.21 to the calibrated value,
   with a caption on why a fixed value fails for some people.
4. **Duration to symbol.** Closures become horizontal bars on a duration axis with coloured bands (reject / dot / dash /
   delete). Bars drop into their band.
5. **Gaps to letters and words.** The symbol sequence groups into letters and words on the tape.
6. **Your turn.** A button to `/try`.

Each step has a text-only fallback with the same content, for reduced motion and screen readers.

## 9. Analysis pipeline (`analysis/`, Python 3.11)

- `load.py`: read `.jsonl` logs and label CSVs; validate the schema; refuse files with missing frames > 5%.
- `replay.py`: run D1–D5 over the logged features (a port of the TS logic, plus a parity test against the TS replay
  output on fixtures, with max difference 0 events).
- `metrics.py`: event matching (IoU ≥ 0.3), F1, false triggers/min, duration error, MSD error rate, CPM.
- `stats.py`: participant bootstrap CIs, Wilcoxon + Holm, κ.
- `figures.py`: F1–F8, matplotlib, a consistent style, colours matching the site tokens.
- `export_site.py`: writes the JSON consumed by `/research`.
- `run.py`: does everything; prints a summary table; writes `analysis/out/summary.json`.
- `pytest` tests for the metrics on hand-made cases.
- `requirements.txt` pinned.

|---|---|
