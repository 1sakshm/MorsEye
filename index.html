Research prototype · webcam Morse text entry

Blink in Morse.
The webcam types it.

MorsEye turns deliberate eye closures into dots and dashes, and learns to ignore the 15–20 blinks a minute you don't mean. A few seconds of personal calibration replace a one-size-fits-all threshold.

Try it Read the paper (PDF)

Runs in your browser. Camera frames never leave your device.

Each closure in this animation lasts 400 ms, inside the dot band (200–600 ms). A letter is committed after 1200 ms with the eyes open. These are the app’s current settings.

Results · a public-video check and one exploratory session

What the data show

“It works on my face” is not a result, so the claim was put to a test. The planned multi-session study stopped after one session without labelled closure times, so its main tests were not run. What remains: other people’s natural blinks in a public video dataset (a secondary, not blind analysis), and one recorded session of the author (exploratory).

Study design, results and the paper

How it works

From camera frame to letter

  1. 1

    Camera

    About 30 frames a second from an ordinary laptop webcam.

  2. 2

    Face landmarks

    MediaPipe finds 478 points on the face, on your device.

  3. 3

    Eye signal

    Eye aspect ratio from six points per eye, or blink scores.

  4. 4

    Detector

    A threshold fitted to you, with hysteresis, finds each closure.

  5. 5

    Segmenter

    The closure’s duration decides: ignore, dot, dash or delete.

  6. 6

    Decoder

    Pauses end letters and words; the text can be spoken aloud.

Closure duration decides the symbol

Spontaneous blinks are short, so anything shorter than the reject window is ignored. These are the starting values; the final ones will be chosen from the Study A duration data.

Honest status

What it is, and what it is not

  • A research prototype. It is being evaluated by one able-bodied adult, its author. It has not been tested with people with motor impairments, and no clinical claim is made.
  • One person. The deliberate-blink and typing results come from the author alone, who is also the labeller, on one laptop camera. A public dataset of four other people checks the natural-blink results.
  • Cued closures. Deliberate blinks in the study are made in response to cues, not from a user’s own intent to write.
  • Webcam timing. At 30 frames a second each frame is 33 ms long, which limits how precisely a closure can be timed.
  • Private by design. No server, accounts or analytics. Study recordings stay on the experimenter’s laptop; only features, never faces, are published.
