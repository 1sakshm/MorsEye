Data

A features-only dataset

Per-frame eye features from one recorded session of cued short, long and winking eye closures and natural blinks while reading, on a consumer laptop webcam. No images, no video, no faces.

Released 25 Oct 2026 under CC BY 4.0, in data/public/ on GitHub, with a full datasheet (data/DATASHEET.md).

  • P01_A_2026-10-24.jsonl (session log, 2.9 MB)
  • P01_A_cue_types.csv (response type for each cue)

What is in it

Per-frame features (one row per video frame)

Per-frame feature columns
ColumnMeaning
t_msFrame capture time in milliseconds
faceWhether a face was found in the frame
earL, earREye aspect ratio of each eye, from six landmarks
blinkL, blinkRMediaPipe blink scores (blendshapes), 0 to 1
yaw, pitch, rollHead pose in degrees
n_facesNumber of faces in view
eye_ptsThe 12 eye landmarks (6 per eye) as normalised x, y; no other face points

Response types (one row per cue)

The participant checked, in the labelling tool, which kind of closure was made in response to each cue. Closure start and end times were not labelled, so these are not detection ground truth.

Response type columns
ColumnMeaning
cue_msWhen the cue appeared, on the frame clock
cue, blockThe cued closure (SHORT, LONG, WINK-L, WINK-R) and its block
response_typeshort, long, wink-L, wink-R, or none
labeller, labelled_atWho checked it, and when

Session information

Cue times and types, block start and end times, the participant ID (P01), age range, glasses, lighting, distance, camera model and the measured frame rate.

What is not in it

  • No images or video, and no face mesh beyond the 12 eye points.
  • No names, contact details or anything else that identifies a participant.
  • No audio.

Datasheet in brief

Purpose
Measure how well a consumer webcam separates deliberate from spontaneous blinks.
Who
One able-bodied adult, the author, in one detection session (planned: 6–8 detection sessions and 5 typing sessions; data collection stopped after the first).
How
A built-in laptop webcam at about 45 cm in front lighting, at 10–16.5 frames per second; about 10 minutes in the app’s study mode, with everything recorded on the laptop.
Labelling
The author checked the response type for each cue by hand; closure onsets and offsets were not labelled.
Intended uses
Research on blink detection, calibration and blink-based text entry.
Not intended for
Identifying people, or any clinical or diagnostic use.
Licence
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). The code is MIT-licensed.

Privacy

  • The only participant is the author, who recorded and publishes the data.
  • Video is recorded on the laptop only to help with labelling, and is never published.
  • Files carry an ID only.
  • The public website and the Try it app record nothing: frames are processed in your browser and never uploaded.
